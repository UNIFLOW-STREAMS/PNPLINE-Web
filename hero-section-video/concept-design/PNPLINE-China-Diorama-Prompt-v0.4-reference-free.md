### 1. Core Concept

Create a **symbolic Chinese waterfront city miniature**, featuring carefully curated Chinese architectural landmarks, compact urban districts, and a small logistics harbor integrated into one coherent three-dimensional environment.

The city should be interpreted as a symbolic representation of China rather than a geographically accurate reconstruction of an actual Chinese city.

The entire environment should feel deliberately composed, architecturally meaningful, and visually sophisticated.

The viewer should first recognize a symbolic Chinese miniature city, then discover the waterfront, logistics port, container ship, and subtle PNPLINE branding.

**Visual hierarchy:**

1. Symbolic Chinese miniature city
2. Recognizable landmark composition
3. Open C-shaped waterfront city structure
4. Compact logistics port and container ship
5. Subtle PNPLINE brand cues

Approximate visual emphasis:

- **City and architecture: 60–70%**
- **Logistics and port: 30–40%**

These proportions are visual composition guidelines, not strict geometric measurements.

### 5. Landmark Treatment

Include four identifiable Chinese landmark forms:

1. **Temple of Heaven — Hall of Prayer for Good Harvests**
2. **Oriental Pearl Tower**
3. **Shanghai Tower**
4. **Guangzhou Tower / Canton Tower**

The landmarks should feel integrated into the urban environment rather than displayed as isolated architectural trophies.

**Landmark modeling principles:**

- Preserve each landmark's recognizable silhouette.
- Retain its defining geometric and structural characteristics.
- Simplify secondary architectural details.
- Reduce excessive window density and tiny surface patterns.
- Avoid photorealistic facade textures.
- Maintain carefully controlled proportions and clean geometry.

**Specific treatment:**

**Oriental Pearl Tower**

- Preserve its distinctive spherical observation volumes.
- Maintain the slender vertical structure and upper spire.
- Simplify surface segmentation without losing its iconic silhouette.

**Shanghai Tower**

- Preserve the twisting, tapering skyscraper form.
- Simplify facade and glazing details.
- Use clean, restrained glass-like color planes.

**Guangzhou Tower**

- Preserve the narrow-waisted twisting structure.
- Maintain visible open lattice geometry.
- Do not simplify the tower into a solid smooth cone or vase.
- Reduce lattice complexity while keeping its structural identity recognizable.

**Temple of Heaven**

- Preserve the circular composition and layered roof structure.
- Maintain the distinctive roof silhouette, supporting columns, and raised base.
- Simplify ornamentation without eliminating its architectural identity.

**Detail hierarchy:**

Higher structural detail:

- Major architectural landmarks
- Their distinctive geometric features
- Main waterfront architectural elements

Lower structural detail:

- Ordinary high-rise and mid-rise buildings
- Background buildings
- Trees and landscaping
- Cars and small boats
- Roads and secondary urban props

**Avoid applying the same level of detail to every building.**

### 7. Color / Lighting / Material

**Color direction**

Use a sophisticated palette based on warm neutrals, muted architectural colors, and restrained accent colors.

**Core color rules**

- Use a bright, warm-neutral base.
- Prioritize low-saturation blues, grays, sand tones, and warm whites.
- Use accent colors sparingly and deliberately.
- Reserve PNPLINE brand blue for selected port and logistics elements only.
- Avoid random, multicolored mosaics of shipping containers in saturated primary colors.

Recommended palette:

- Background: bright warm neutral / warm greige
- Buildings: light gray, warm stone, off-white, muted blue-gray
- Glass elements: simplified pale blue-gray or smoky glass tones
- Traditional architecture: muted terracotta, restrained teal, soft warm brown
- Greenery: muted natural green
- Port structures: neutral gray with selective PNPLINE blue accents
- Containers: carefully grouped neutral colors and limited brand blue

Avoid excessive saturation, random primary-color accents, or candy-like pastel combinations.

The palette should feel refined and curated rather than playful or decorative.

**Material direction**

Use clean, simplified miniature materials:

- Matte-to-satin surface finishes
- Smooth painted architectural surfaces
- Simplified glass reflections
- Clean geometric edges
- Subtle material differentiation
- Low texture noise
- Minimal weathering

Stone, glass, painted metal, and water should remain visually distinguishable without relying on photorealistic texture complexity.

Avoid excessively glossy plastic surfaces.

**Water direction**

The water must be stylized, smooth, and neatly organized.

**The water should be rendered as a sleek, clean, stylized surface rather than with realistic wave detail.**

Use broad, controlled reflections and subtle color variation.

Avoid realistic choppy waves, turbulent foam, strong ocean ripples, or physically elaborate water simulation.

The water should feel like an intentionally designed part of the miniature diorama rather than a realistic ocean inserted into the scene.

**Lighting direction**

- Bright, clean, soft daylight
- Studio-like premium presentation lighting
- Gentle directional illumination
- Crisp but soft shadows
- Clear separation between lit and shaded surfaces
- Subtle ambient occlusion to reveal architectural structure
- Controlled highlights
- Consistent lighting across the entire diorama

Lighting should emphasize shape, proportion, and architectural depth.

Avoid dramatic sunset lighting, cinematic fog, excessive bloom, strong atmospheric perspective, or a photorealistic HDR outdoor look.
