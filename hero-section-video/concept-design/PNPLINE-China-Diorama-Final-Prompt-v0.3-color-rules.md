# PNPLINE — Chinese Symbolic City & Port Miniature Diorama
## Final Concept Design Generation Prompt v0.3

### Task / Goal

Create **one final concept design image** of a Chinese symbolic city-and-port miniature diorama for PNPLINE Scroll World.

The goal is to create a premium, visually rich, stylized miniature representation of China that combines recognizable architectural landmarks, a dense but carefully curated urban environment, a waterfront, and a compact logistics port.

The image must not resemble a photorealistic city skyline, realistic port visualization, or conventional architectural rendering.

**Reference image priorities:**

- **Image A — Layout reference:** Defines the spatial arrangement, open C-shaped waterfront city layout, landmark positions, port location, ship placement, and open-sea direction.
- **Images B, C, D — Primary style references:** Define the graphic miniature aesthetic, simplified architectural forms, color treatment, material language, lighting, and selective detail hierarchy.
- **Image E — Optional secondary reference:** Use only as an additional reference for geometric simplification, without introducing excessive toy-like characteristics.

**Critical instruction:**

Use Image A for layout fidelity and Images B–D for style fidelity. Preserve the spatial arrangement of Image A while completely reinterpreting its visual treatment according to the premium stylized miniature language of Images B–D.

### 1. Core Concept

Create a **symbolic Chinese waterfront city miniature**, featuring carefully curated Chinese architectural landmarks, compact urban districts, and a small logistics harbor integrated into one coherent three-dimensional environment.

The city should be interpreted as a symbolic representation of China rather than a geographically accurate reconstruction of an actual Chinese city.

The entire environment should feel deliberately composed, architecturally meaningful, and visually sophisticated.

The viewer should first recognize a symbolic Chinese miniature city, then discover the waterfront, logistics port, container ship, and subtle PNPLINE branding.

**Visual hierarchy:**

1. Symbolic Chinese miniature city
2. Recognizable landmark composition
3. Open C-shaped waterfront city structure
4. Compact logistics port and container ship
5. Subtle PNPLINE brand cues

Approximate visual emphasis:

- **City and architecture: 60–70%**
- **Logistics and port: 30–40%**

These proportions are visual composition guidelines, not strict geometric measurements.

### 2. Overall Style Direction

The image must follow a **premium stylized 3D miniature diorama aesthetic** rather than a realistic architectural visualization.

The overall look should be:

- Simplified, graphic, and clean
- Dense and visually rich, but not cluttered
- Elegant, curated, and architecturally sophisticated
- Symbolic rather than geographically realistic
- Highly organized in shape, color, and composition
- Clearly miniature in appearance without looking childish
- Rich in meaningful architectural forms without excessive surface detail

Use an intentional balance between geometric simplification and structural accuracy.

Ordinary buildings should be simplified into clean architectural masses, while important landmarks should retain enough distinctive structural detail to remain recognizable.

**The goal is not to make everything low-poly. The goal is to simplify forms selectively while preserving recognizable architectural structure.**

### 5. Landmark Treatment

Include four identifiable Chinese landmark forms:

1. **Temple of Heaven — Hall of Prayer for Good Harvests**
2. **Oriental Pearl Tower**
3. **Shanghai Tower**
4. **Guangzhou Tower / Canton Tower**

The landmarks should feel integrated into the urban environment rather than displayed as isolated architectural trophies.

**Landmark modeling principles:**

- Preserve each landmark's recognizable silhouette.
- Retain its defining geometric and structural characteristics.
- Simplify secondary architectural details.
- Reduce excessive window density and tiny surface patterns.
- Avoid photorealistic facade textures.
- Maintain carefully controlled proportions and clean geometry.

**Specific treatment:**

**Oriental Pearl Tower**

- Preserve its distinctive spherical observation volumes.
- Maintain the slender vertical structure and upper spire.
- Simplify surface segmentation without losing its iconic silhouette.

**Shanghai Tower**

- Preserve the twisting, tapering skyscraper form.
- Simplify facade and glazing details.
- Use clean, restrained glass-like color planes.

**Guangzhou Tower**

- Preserve the narrow-waisted twisting structure.
- Maintain visible open lattice geometry.
- Do not simplify the tower into a solid smooth cone or vase.
- Reduce lattice complexity while keeping its structural identity recognizable.

**Temple of Heaven**

- Preserve the circular composition and layered roof structure.
- Maintain the distinctive roof silhouette, supporting columns, and raised base.
- Simplify ornamentation without eliminating its architectural identity.

**Detail hierarchy:**

Higher structural detail:

- Major architectural landmarks
- Their distinctive geometric features
- Main waterfront architectural elements

Lower structural detail:

- Ordinary high-rise and mid-rise buildings
- Background buildings
- Trees and landscaping
- Cars and small boats
- Roads and secondary urban props

**Avoid applying the same level of detail to every building.**

### 6. Port / Logistics Treatment

The port must be interpreted as a **compact, stylized logistics district integrated into the miniature city**, not a full-scale industrial port simulation.

Include:

- One clearly recognizable container ship
- Two simplified but structurally readable port cranes
- Organized container groups
- A compact quay
- One low logistics building
- Minimal supporting port structures

**Container ship:**

- Preserve a clean, elongated hull silhouette.
- Clearly distinguish the bow, stern, deck, bridge, and container loading area.
- Simplify secondary ship equipment and surface details.
- Do not make the ship look short, rounded, or toy-like.

**Port cranes:**

- Preserve the main support structure, boom, and lifting mechanism.
- Use simple, clean geometric forms.
- Avoid excessive cables, ladders, and mechanical micro-details.

**Containers:**

- Use clean rectangular volumes.
- Preserve enough structural edges and corrugation to remain identifiable.
- Organize containers into coherent color groups.
- Avoid random multicolored container mosaics.

**Brand application:**

Apply subtle PNPLINE brand cues only to selected logistics elements, such as:

- Selected containers
- Ship color blocking
- Small port structures
- Limited areas of the logistics building

Use PNPLINE Blue `#00ABE1` selectively, supported where appropriate by `#007FA8`, white, and neutral tones.

**Do not paint the entire city in PNPLINE's brand blue.**

Do not transform Chinese landmarks into branded objects.

Do not imply that PNPLINE owns the depicted ship, port, or facilities.

If an approved PNPLINE logo asset is not provided, use brand color blocking without inventing logo text or symbols.

### 7. Color / Lighting / Material

**Color direction**

Use a sophisticated palette based on warm neutrals, muted architectural colors, and restrained accent colors.

**Core color rules**

- Use a bright, warm-neutral base.
- Prioritize low-saturation blues, grays, sand tones, and warm whites.
- Use accent colors sparingly and deliberately.
- Reserve PNPLINE brand blue for selected port and logistics elements only.
- Avoid random, multicolored mosaics of shipping containers in saturated primary colors.

Recommended palette:

- Background: bright warm neutral / warm greige
- Buildings: light gray, warm stone, off-white, muted blue-gray
- Glass elements: simplified pale blue-gray or smoky glass tones
- Traditional architecture: muted terracotta, restrained teal, soft warm brown
- Greenery: muted natural green
- Port structures: neutral gray with selective PNPLINE blue accents
- Containers: carefully grouped neutral colors and limited brand blue

Avoid excessive saturation, random primary-color accents, or candy-like pastel combinations.

The palette should feel refined and curated rather than playful or decorative.

**Material direction**

Use clean, simplified miniature materials:

- Matte-to-satin surface finishes
- Smooth painted architectural surfaces
- Simplified glass reflections
- Clean geometric edges
- Subtle material differentiation
- Low texture noise
- Minimal weathering

Stone, glass, painted metal, and water should remain visually distinguishable without relying on photorealistic texture complexity.

Avoid excessively glossy plastic surfaces.

**Water direction**

The water must be stylized, smooth, and neatly organized.

**The water should be rendered as a sleek, clean, stylized surface rather than with realistic wave detail.**

Use broad, controlled reflections and subtle color variation.

Avoid realistic choppy waves, turbulent foam, strong ocean ripples, or physically elaborate water simulation.

The water should feel like an intentionally designed part of the miniature diorama rather than a realistic ocean inserted into the scene.

**Lighting direction**

- Bright, clean, soft daylight
- Studio-like premium presentation lighting
- Gentle directional illumination
- Crisp but soft shadows
- Clear separation between lit and shaded surfaces
- Subtle ambient occlusion to reveal architectural structure
- Controlled highlights
- Consistent lighting across the entire diorama

Lighting should emphasize shape, proportion, and architectural depth.

Avoid dramatic sunset lighting, cinematic fog, excessive bloom, strong atmospheric perspective, or a photorealistic HDR outdoor look.

### 8. Negative Prompts / Things to Avoid

**Avoid the following visual results:**

**Photorealism and excessive realism**

- Photorealistic skyline
- Realistic port visualization
- Real-world aerial photography
- Live-action cinematic CG appearance
- Highly realistic architectural rendering
- Over-detailed urban simulation
- Gritty industrial realism
- Dirty or weathered industrial materials
- Hyper-realistic water simulation

**Excessive simplification or toy-like appearance**

- Cheap plastic toy appearance
- Cartoonishly cute buildings
- Theme-park souvenir model appearance
- Excessively rounded architectural forms
- Oversimplified landmark silhouettes
- Identical material treatment across all objects

**Composition errors**

- Straight-line arrangement of landmarks
- Isolated trophy-like landmark presentation
- Perfectly symmetrical semicircular city
- Closed circular harbor
- Port structures dominating the city
- Large empty water areas separating the city from the port
- A blocked or visually closed sea exit
- Unrelated objects scattered across the base

**Color and material errors**

- Painting the entire city in PNPLINE's brand blue
- Oversaturated primary colors
- Random multicolored container mosaics
- Excessive glossy plastic materials
- Hyper-detailed surface textures
- Random repetitive window details
- Strong glow, bloom, or cinematic haze

**Text and graphic errors**

- Oversized text
- Giant CHINA letters
- City-name typography
- Labels, arrows, numbers, legends, or infographic overlays
- Website UI or interface elements
- Chinese national flags or emblems
- Invented commercial logos or unreadable text

Do not copy the specific cities, landmarks, architectural arrangements, logos, or branded compositions from Images B–D. Use them only as visual style references.
